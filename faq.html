Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions about what these pages are, why they exist, how they were made, and how to send corrections or support sanctuary foxes.

1

Are these pages a course?

No. These pages are not a course. They are concepts, explanations, patterns, warnings, and practical field notes that people who want to use AI should familiarize themselves with. Think of them as a map of useful ideas, not an enrollment path.

2

Why did you write these pages?

Because I could, and because sharing knowledge is usually more useful than keeping lessons locked in one person’s notebook. I like helping people understand practical AI use so they do not have to repeat avoidable mistakes, rediscover the same lessons, or learn the hard way when the blast radius gets teeth.

3

Do you use tracking cookies or otherwise track visitors?

Nope. These pages do not use tracking cookies, analytics pixels, ad beacons, forms, or visitor-monitoring scripts.

4

What did you use to generate these pages?

The pages were created with help from ChatGPT and OpenAI Codex, with human direction, review, editing, and taste checks along the way. The tools helped draft, structure, and refine the material. The responsibility for what gets published still belongs to a person.

5

Can I donate to support the sanctuary foxes?

Yes. Donations can be sent through PayPal. All proceeds from that donation link go to feeding non-releasable sanctuary foxes.

6

I do not agree with one of your pages. What should I do?

Okay. Email me at jterlecki@gmail.com and I will review it. Please include the page title, the section or sentence you are concerned about, and what you think should be corrected, clarified, or updated.

Helpful feedback

What makes a correction easier to review?

Specific feedback is easier to act on than a general objection. Send the page name, the exact sentence or claim, why you think it is wrong or misleading, and any source, example, or practical experience that supports the correction. That turns the email from fog into a flashlight.

  • Best signal. “This sentence is outdated because the tool changed on May 10, 2026, and the current documentation says...”
  • Also useful. “This section is confusing because beginners may think it gives permission to use AI on sensitive data.”
  • Less useful. “I hate this page.” Colorful, yes. Operationally slippery.

Field guide takeaway: This FAQ is the front desk, not the course catalog. These pages share concepts, boundaries, and practical lessons so visitors can think more clearly before AI turns into process, code, policy, or operational habit.
