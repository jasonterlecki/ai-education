Infrastructure and IaC Risk

Infrastructure changes are not just code. They are operational reality with a text interface, and AI-assisted changes need stricter controls.

Expertise: Advanced Audience: Cloud, platform, and security teams Use when: AI touches infrastructure or deployment automation Version: 1.0
Back to index Previous: Code Review with AI Next: Security and Governance

Who this is for

This guide is for teams using AI around Terraform, Kubernetes, IAM, DNS, networking, certificates, deployment automation, and other operational systems.

What you will learn

  • Why IaC has a larger blast radius than normal code.
  • Which controls are required before applying changes.
  • How to separate planning, dry runs, review, and rollback.

Risk ladder

LowDocumentation or non-applied examples.
MediumNon-production manifests, local scripts, or test environments.
HighTerraform state, Kubernetes services, DNS, certificates, deployments.
CriticalIAM, production networking, secrets, payment, regulated workloads.

Required controls

Blast radius

Know what systems, users, and environments can be affected.

Stateful systems

Treat state, dependencies, and drift as first-class risks.

Least privilege

Do not grant broad permissions for convenience.

Plan and dry-run

Use terraform plan, diff, dry-run, or preview steps before apply.

Environment separation

Keep dev, test, and production boundaries explicit.

Protected branches

Require review before operational changes can merge or run.

Rollback plans

Define restore paths before applying infrastructure changes.

Secrets handling

Never paste secrets or expose them in prompts, logs, or diffs.

Good use and risky use

Good use

Use AI to explain IaC, draft safer plans, identify risks, and prepare review questions before humans run dry-run, peer review, and apply steps.

Risky use

Let AI directly modify and apply Terraform, IAM, Kubernetes, DNS, certificates, or deployment automation without dry runs and approvals.

Try this prompt

Use this before changing infrastructure.

Review this infrastructure change for blast radius, state impact, least-privilege concerns, environment boundaries, secrets handling, dry-run requirements, peer review, and rollback. Do not suggest applying changes. List validation commands and approvals needed before apply.

Review checklist

Plan: Was a dry-run or preview reviewed? Permissions: Does the change follow least privilege? State: Are state, dependencies, and drift understood? Secrets: Were secrets excluded from prompts, logs, and diffs? Rollback: Is recovery documented and tested where possible?

Key takeaway

AI can help reason about infrastructure. It should not shortcut operational controls that protect real environments.
