AI Operating Model

An AI operating model turns enthusiasm into repeatable, accountable practice.

Expertise: Expert Audience: AI committees and governance owners Use when: Defining who can use AI, where, and under which controls Version: 1.0
Back to index Next: AI Architecture Strategy

Who this is for

This guide is for leaders who must convert AI interest into clear roles, approved tools, data boundaries, risk tiers, and accountable outcomes.

What you will learn

  • How to define practical AI use rights instead of vague encouragement.
  • How review gates, escalation paths, and ownership reduce unmanaged risk.
  • Why tool approval and data classification belong in the operating model.

Strategic frame

A policy that says "use AI responsibly" is not an operating model. A usable operating model says who may use which tools, for which work, with what data, under which review process, and who owns the final outcome.

The model should be understandable by employees, enforceable by managers, testable by security teams, and adaptable as tools and regulations change.

Operating model building blocks

Roles

Separate casual users, power users, technical implementers, reviewers, approvers, and governance owners.

Approved tools

Name which tools are approved for each class of work and data.

Data classification

Connect public, internal, confidential, restricted, regulated, and secret data to AI use rules.

Risk tiers

Classify AI work by impact on people, money, systems, security, and compliance.

Review gates

Require human review, testing, legal, security, or executive approval where risk demands it.

Escalation paths

Give employees a place to ask when the right answer is not obvious.

Good use and risky use

Good use

Publish clear use tiers, approved tools, data rules, review gates, and accountable owners so teams can move quickly inside known boundaries.

Risky use

Tell teams to innovate with AI while leaving tool approval, data handling, and review responsibility ambiguous.

Decision framework

QuestionLow-control answerRequired leadership answer
Who may use AI?Anyone, any way.Role-based use rights with training expectations.
Which data is allowed?User judgment only.Rules tied to data classification and approved tools.
Who owns output?The tool or vendor.The person, team, and approval chain using the result.
When does work escalate?Only after failure.Predefined triggers for legal, security, privacy, or executive review.

Leadership questions

1. Which AI uses are encouraged, restricted, or prohibited? 2. Which tools are approved for each data class? 3. Who can approve exceptions? 4. What evidence proves that review happened? 5. Who is accountable when AI-assisted work causes harm or error?

Governance checklist

Roles: Document who may draft, approve, automate, administer, and audit AI use. Controls: Connect risk tiers to review gates, logging, and escalation paths. Data: State which data classes are allowed in which tools. Ownership: Make outcome ownership explicit for every approved use pattern. Maintenance: Review the model as tools, vendors, regulations, and business uses change.

Key takeaway

An AI operating model is the management system for AI use. It makes safe practice repeatable instead of relying on individual interpretation.
