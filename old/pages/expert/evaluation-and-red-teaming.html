Evaluation and Red Teaming

AI quality cannot be managed by vibes. It needs repeatable evaluation.

Expertise: Expert Audience: Evaluators, risk owners, and security leaders Use when: An AI system must be trusted for repeatable work Version: 1.0
Back to index Previous: AI Architecture Strategy Next: Retrieval and Knowledge Governance

Who this is for

This guide is for leaders and evaluators who need evidence before approving AI use in business, technical, customer, or governance workflows.

What you will learn

  • How to evaluate AI against task-specific expectations.
  • Why human review, safety testing, and red-team scenarios complement metrics.
  • How acceptance thresholds prevent vague approval.

Strategic frame

Evaluation should test the actual job the AI is expected to perform, not generic intelligence. A useful program combines expected-answer test sets, human review, hallucination checks, prompt injection testing, bias and fairness review, regression testing, and defined acceptance thresholds.

Evaluation stack

Task test sets

Use examples that reflect real work, edge cases, and known failure modes.

Expected answers

Define what good, partial, and unacceptable output look like.

Human review

Use accountable reviewers for judgment, tone, policy, and business fit.

Safety testing

Test harmful, sensitive, misleading, or unauthorized output paths.

Prompt injection

Check whether hostile content can override instructions or trigger unsafe actions.

Regression tests

Repeat tests after model, prompt, retrieval, or tool changes.

Good use and risky use

Good use

Approve AI workflows after measured performance, human review, red-team scenarios, hallucination checks, and explicit acceptance thresholds.

Risky use

Trust a polished demo, a few successful prompts, or vendor claims without testing failure modes, regression risk, and business-specific accuracy.

Decision framework

LowSample review and clear human ownership.Useful drafts with low consequence of error.

HighSafety tests, red-team cases, regression tests, audit evidence.Meets threshold and has controls for failure.

Leadership questions

1. What task is being evaluated, and what does success mean? 2. Which examples represent edge cases, abuse cases, and normal work? 3. What hallucination, bias, fairness, or safety failures are unacceptable? 4. What threshold must be met before rollout? 5. How will we retest after prompts, models, data, tools, or policies change?

Governance checklist

Test data: Maintain representative test sets with expected outcomes. Review: Define reviewer roles and scoring rubrics. Red team: Include prompt injection, unsafe actions, data leakage, and brittle workflow cases. Thresholds: Set launch, pause, rollback, and retest criteria. Evidence: Keep evaluation records for audit and future comparison.

Key takeaway

Evaluation makes AI governance concrete. It replaces confidence with evidence and turns red-team findings into safer design.
