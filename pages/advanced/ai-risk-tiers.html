AI Risk Tiers

AI risk tiers turn “is this safe?” into a practical routing decision. A tier model helps teams decide which use cases are simple productivity aids, which need review, which need controlled rollout, and which should not proceed without executive, legal, security, privacy, or operational ownership.

Use case intake Governance routing Blast-radius control

Risk tiers are traffic control, not paperwork cosplay.

A good tier model does not slow every idea to a committee crawl. It separates low-risk experimentation from sensitive, automated, customer-impacting, regulated, or hard-to-reverse work. The goal is to make the easy things easy, the risky things reviewed, and the unacceptable things obviously blocked.

The tier should follow the use case, data, autonomy, impact, reversibility, and exposure. It should not follow the excitement level of the demo.

The tier asks three blunt questions

1
What can go wrong?Bad advice, wrong decision, data leak, unsafe action, customer harm, legal exposure, production outage, or reputational confetti storm.
2
Can a human catch it?The risk changes when AI output is reviewed before use, compared with being sent, merged, deployed, or acted on automatically.
3
Can we recover?Reversibility matters. A draft can be deleted. A production permission change, customer message, or HR decision has sharper teeth.

The six dimensions that drive the tier

Do not classify risk by tool name alone. The same model can be low risk for brainstorming and high risk when connected to private data, operational tools, or consequential decisions.

Data sensitivity

Public, internal, confidential, regulated, personal, customer, health, financial, legal, security, or secret-bearing data.

Decision impact

Whether the output influences access, employment, money, eligibility, safety, legal position, customer treatment, or operational priority.

Autonomy

Whether AI only suggests, drafts, edits, executes, sends, deploys, deletes, approves, or triggers downstream automation.

Exposure

Whether the result stays personal, reaches a team, affects customers, enters production, or becomes public record.

Reversibility

Whether mistakes can be corrected cheaply, require rollback, create audit issues, or cause harm that cannot be un-sent.

System access

Whether the workflow connects to files, repos, tickets, email, databases, cloud APIs, MCP tools, admin consoles, or production systems.

A practical internal tier model

This model is not a legal classification by itself. It is an operational triage system that helps teams decide what review, evidence, controls, and ownership must exist before a use case moves forward.

Personal learning and exploration

Low-stakes personal use where the output does not touch company data, customers, production systems, financial commitments, legal claims, or employment decisions.

  • Brainstorming public ideas.
  • Learning a concept.
  • Drafting a personal outline.
No sensitive dataNo automationHuman use only

Productivity assist

AI helps an individual or team draft, summarize, rephrase, classify, or explain work, with human review before the result is relied on or shared.

  • Meeting summaries.
  • Draft emails.
  • Internal document review.
Human reviewLow blast radiusNo direct action

Team workflow support

AI supports repeatable internal work using internal context, but outputs are reviewed by the responsible owner before they affect shared systems or decisions.

  • Code review support.
  • Ticket triage suggestions.
  • Knowledge base drafts.
Internal dataOwner reviewEvidence required

Business process influence

AI influences operational workflows, customer-facing content, prioritization, risk scoring, technical changes, or decisions that people may act on.

  • Customer support recommendations.
  • Release notes and public pages.
  • Implementation plans and rollout guidance.
Approval gateTestingAudit trail

High-impact or sensitive decision support

AI touches regulated, personal, financial, legal, security, infrastructure, employment, safety, or other sensitive domains where errors can materially affect people or systems.

  • Security incident triage.
  • Infrastructure and IAM recommendations.
  • Eligibility, risk, or compliance analysis.
Named ownerFormal reviewMonitoring

Blocked, prohibited, or executive exception

AI would make or execute consequential decisions without appropriate human authority, use unacceptable practices, bypass consent, or perform destructive, privileged, or hard-to-reverse actions.

  • Autonomous hiring or denial decisions.
  • Production destructive actions without approval.
  • Secret extraction or covert surveillance.
Do not proceedExecutive reviewLegal escalation

Controls by tier

The higher the tier, the more the workflow needs explicit ownership, validation evidence, auditability, and stop points. A tier without controls is just a colored sticker on a moving forklift.

Tier Default approval Data rules Tool and automation rules Evidence required
T0 Individual judgment. Public or non-sensitive information only. No connected internal tools or actions. None beyond normal personal review.
T1 Human review before use. Internal data allowed only if permitted by policy and tool terms. No autonomous write, send, merge, or deploy. Reviewed output, source notes, and known uncertainty.
T2 Team owner review. Least necessary internal context, with masking for sensitive fields. Read-only or draft-only tools preferred. Sources, assumptions, diff or draft, and reviewer decision.
T3 Process owner approval. Classified data handling, retention rules, and source-of-truth checks. Write actions need exact approval and rollback path. Validation, acceptance criteria, change record, and monitoring plan.
T4 Security, privacy, legal, compliance, or executive review as applicable. Sensitive data minimization, access controls, logging, retention limits, and privacy review. Scoped tools, step-up approvals, sandboxing, and separation of duties. Risk assessment, test results, audit trail, owner sign-off, rollback, and incident path.
T5 Blocked unless formally approved as an exception. Do not process until lawful basis, authority, and controls are established. No autonomous action. No destructive or consequential execution. Formal exception record, named accountability, legal basis, and independent review.

What raises the tier?

Escalation is not punishment. It is the governance equivalent of putting on gloves before handling the spiky machinery.

↑

Sensitive data appears

Customer data, employee data, secrets, security logs, contracts, financial data, health information, or regulated records push the use case upward.

↑

AI influences a decision

If people rely on the output to approve, deny, prioritize, investigate, discipline, price, diagnose, or escalate, the tier rises.

↑

Tools can change state

Writing, sending, merging, deploying, deleting, creating tickets, editing records, or calling APIs turns advice into action.

↑

Rollback is hard

Public messages, customer impact, production changes, access changes, payments, and human-impacting decisions need stricter review.

Example use case routing

The tier is contextual. A “summary” can be T1 when it rewrites public notes, T3 when it becomes customer-facing, and T4 when it summarizes a security incident with sensitive logs.

Rewrite a public blog paragraph

T1

Low-stakes drafting, as long as a human checks tone, accuracy, claims, and brand fit before publishing.

Summarize internal meeting notes

T2

Internal context is involved. The summary needs human review, especially for decisions, owners, dates, and commitments.

Suggest customer support replies

T3

Customer-facing impact means approval, source grounding, policy checks, and clear limits on what AI may promise.

Review Terraform or IAM changes

T4

Infrastructure changes can affect access, cost, availability, and security. AI can assist, but humans own approval and execution.

Auto-rank candidates for hiring

T4 or T5

Employment impact, bias risk, and legal obligations demand formal review, strict controls, and possibly a hard stop.

Autonomously delete production data

T5

This should be blocked unless there is an explicit, approved, auditable, reversible operational procedure with human command authority.

Governance artifacts that make tiers operational

A tier model only works when it shows up in intake, reviews, tools, and approvals. Otherwise it becomes a laminated dragon, impressive but not especially helpful.

1

Use case intake

Capture purpose, users, data, tools, outputs, autonomy, review path, system access, and expected business value.

2

Risk tier record

Record the assigned tier, rationale, escalation triggers, required controls, owner, approver, and review date.

3

Control checklist

Map the tier to data handling, tool restrictions, human review, logging, testing, monitoring, and rollback expectations.

4

Exception process

Define who can approve blocked or high-risk work, what evidence is required, and when the exception expires.

5

Monitoring plan

Track error reports, drift, user behavior, approval bypasses, model changes, tool changes, and data source changes.

6

Retirement path

Governance includes shutting down stale, unsafe, unused, or superseded AI workflows before they become attic automation.

Use this before a pilot, tool rollout, MCP integration, agent workflow, or business process automation.

Recommended tier from T0 to T5. 2. Reasons based on data sensitivity, decision impact, autonomy, exposure, reversibility, and system access. 3. Required controls before approval. 4. What would lower the tier. 5. What would raise the tier. 6. Required owner and approver. 7. Stop conditions that should block the use case.

Field guide takeaway: AI risk tiers are how governance tells the difference between a harmless drafting helper and a workflow that can affect people, data, money, infrastructure, customers, or legal obligations. Classify the use case before the rollout, tie each tier to concrete controls, and make escalation normal before a shiny demo becomes an unmanaged system with a badge and a login.
