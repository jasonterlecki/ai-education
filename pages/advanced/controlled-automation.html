Controlled Automation

The more autonomy AI receives, the more observability, control, and accountability the system needs.

Expertise: Advanced Audience: Automation and technical owners Use when: AI is allowed to act, not just advise Version: 1.0
Back to index Previous: Security and Governance

Who this is for

This guide is for people designing workflows where AI may draft, accelerate, approve, trigger, modify, route, or execute work.

What you will learn

  • The difference between assistance, acceleration, and automation.
  • Which controls increase as autonomy increases.
  • When automation should not be used.

Autonomy levels

AI assistanceAI drafts or analyzes. Humans decide and act.
AI accelerationAI prepares work inside human review and approval gates.
Human-on-the-loopAI acts within limits while humans monitor and can intervene.
Autonomous workflowAI acts without routine approval. Risk controls must be strongest.

Required controls

Human-in-the-loop

A person approves before high-impact action.

Human-on-the-loop

A person monitors automated actions and can stop them.

Approval gates

Use explicit approval before external, financial, security, or production effects.

Monitoring

Observe success, failures, drift, volume, and unusual behavior.

Rollback

Keep recovery paths ready before automation acts.

Alerting

Notify owners quickly when thresholds, failures, or policy limits are hit.

Audit trails

Record inputs, decisions, actions, tools, and human approvals.

No automation zones

Do not automate where ambiguity, regulation, harm, or irreversibility is high.

Good use and risky use

Good use

Use AI to draft, triage, or prepare actions while humans approve higher-risk steps and monitoring catches failures quickly.

Risky use

Let AI autonomously affect users, money, security, production, or regulated workflows without approval gates, monitoring, rollback, or audit trails.

Try this prompt

Use this when evaluating an AI workflow.

Classify this workflow as AI assistance, AI acceleration, human-on-the-loop automation, or autonomous automation: [workflow]. Identify required approval gates, monitoring, rollback, alerting, audit trails, and no-automation zones. Explain where human accountability remains and what should stop the workflow.

Review checklist

Autonomy: Is the AI advising, preparing, or acting? Approvals: Are high-impact actions gated? Observability: Are monitoring, alerts, and audit trails present? Recovery: Can the workflow be stopped and rolled back? Boundaries: Are no-automation zones documented?

Key takeaway

Automation is a control problem. Increase autonomy only when observability, intervention, rollback, and accountability increase with it.
