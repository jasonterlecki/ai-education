Hallucinations and Verification

AI can sound confident and still be wrong. Learn how to verify facts, dates, numbers, names, sources, and assumptions.

Expertise: Beginner Audience: General business users Use when: AI output contains claims or facts Version: 1.0.1

Who this is for

This guide is for anyone using AI output in an email, report, presentation, meeting note, customer message, or decision memo.

What you will learn

  • What an AI hallucination is.
  • Why confident wording is not proof.
  • How to verify claims before using them.

Confidence is not the same as correctness

An AI hallucination is output that appears plausible but is false, unsupported, outdated, or incorrectly inferred. The answer may have polished wording, organized bullets, and a confident tone while still containing errors.

Verification is the step that turns AI-assisted work into responsible work. The more important the output, the more carefully you should check it.

Verification checklist

Check facts: Confirm claims against trusted sources. Check dates: Make sure timelines, deadlines, and recent events are current. Check names: Verify people, teams, products, organizations, and titles. Check numbers: Recalculate totals, percentages, counts, and comparisons. Check sources: Prefer primary or approved sources when accuracy matters. Check assumptions: Identify what the AI filled in without evidence. Ask what is missing: Look for omitted risks, exceptions, or context.

Good use and risky use

Good use

Use AI to create a draft or checklist, then verify claims with trusted sources before forwarding, publishing, deciding, or acting.

Risky use

Use AI-generated facts, legal wording, compliance statements, calculations, or source citations without checking them.

Try this prompt

Use this after AI gives you an answer that includes claims.

Review your previous answer for verification risk. Create a checklist of facts, dates, names, numbers, sources, and assumptions that I should verify before using it. Separate high-risk claims from low-risk wording suggestions. Do not invent sources.

Key takeaway

AI output is a starting point. Verification is what makes it usable for real communication, analysis, and decisions.
