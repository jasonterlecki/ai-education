Human Accountability

AI can assist with work, but people remain responsible for what is sent, approved, changed, or decided.

Expertise: Beginner Audience: General business users Use when: You are using AI output in real work Version: 1.0
Back to index Previous: Hallucinations and Verification

Who this is for

This guide is for anyone who uses AI to help draft, summarize, analyze, communicate, plan, or make recommendations.

What you will learn

  • Why AI does not own outcomes.
  • Where human review is required.
  • How to use AI help without losing accountability.

The person using the output owns the result

AI can help generate options, wording, summaries, and suggestions. It cannot understand your full responsibility, the approval chain, the business context, or the impact on people affected by the work.

If you send it, approve it, publish it, change it, or rely on it, you are responsible for checking whether it is accurate, appropriate, authorized, and complete.

Examples of accountable review

Emails: Check tone, recipients, facts, promises, and private details before sending. Reports: Verify numbers, sources, dates, assumptions, and conclusions. Code suggestions: Use trained technical review, tests, and change control before applying to real systems. Customer communications: Confirm accuracy, policy alignment, and approval requirements. Decisions: Treat AI as input, not as the decision-maker.

Good use and risky use

Good use

Use AI to prepare a draft, compare options, or find review questions. Keep a human in charge of accuracy, approvals, and final judgment.

Risky use

Forward AI output as final, blame the tool for errors, skip required review, or let AI make decisions that affect people, money, systems, or policy.

Try this workflow

Use this before relying on AI-assisted work.

1. Label the output as a draft until reviewed. 2. Check facts, tone, privacy, approvals, and missing context. 3. Ask: "Who is affected if this is wrong?" 4. Ask: "What official source, reviewer, or process should confirm this?" 5. Edit the output yourself before sending or using it.

Key takeaway

AI can share the workload, but it does not share accountability. Human judgment stays in the loop.
