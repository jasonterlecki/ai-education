Laws and Local Rules

Rules about using AI, and about what AI helps you make, are not the same everywhere, and they keep changing. This page does not tell you what is allowed in any place. It reminds you to check before you rely on AI for work.

What this page is, and is not

It is a reminder that the rules differ and change. It is not legal advice, and it does not describe the rules of any country or region. Nobody can summarize every place a reader might be, so this guide does not try.

Things that may differ from place to place

Depending on where you are and who you work for, there may be different rules about:

  • Whether and how AI tools may be used for certain kinds of work.
  • How personal, customer, or confidential information may be handled.
  • Who owns or may reuse what AI helps produce, including copyright and similar questions.
  • Whether AI-assisted content must be labeled or disclosed.
  • Recording, transcribing, or analyzing people’s voices, images, or messages.
  • Using AI to help make decisions about people.

This list is a prompt for your own questions, not a statement of what any place requires.

Before you rely on AI for work

  1. Find out whether your organization has an AI policy, and read it.
  2. Ask which tools and account types are approved for the kind of work you do.
  3. Check the rules that apply where you and the people you serve are located.
  4. Check any client, contract, or school terms that cover your work.
  5. Note which tool you used and when, in case someone asks later.
  6. Check again from time to time. Rules and policies change.
  7. If you are unsure, stop and ask before you continue.

Who to ask

Start with your manager or the person who owns AI tools in your organization. For questions about rules, ask the people responsible for legal, compliance, privacy, or security. If your organization has none of these, ask whoever is accountable for the decision you are about to make. Do not use an AI tool as the final answer on a rules question.
