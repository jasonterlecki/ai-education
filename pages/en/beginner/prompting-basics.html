Prompting Basics

Use goal, context, constraints, examples, and output format to get responses people can actually review. A good prompt is not a magic spell. It is a clean work order for a very fast assistant that needs to know what good looks like before it starts painting the wall.

The core idea

Prompting is how you turn a vague wish into reviewable work. The more clearly you describe the outcome, the situation, the boundaries, examples of what you mean, and the shape of the answer, the less the AI has to guess. Less guessing means fewer shiny wrong answers, fewer irrelevant paragraphs, and fewer responses that sound helpful but cannot be used.

Prompting is not about controlling every word. It is about giving AI enough direction that a human can inspect the result without digging through fog.

Three prompt instincts

1 Say the job.Do not start with the artifact. Start with the purpose: explain, compare, draft, summarize, review, plan, or transform.
2 Show the room.Give the audience, background, source material, risks, and decisions that the answer must respect.
3 Define reviewability.Ask for sections, tables, bullets, assumptions, verification points, or a checklist so people can examine the answer quickly.

The five ingredients

These are the building blocks of a prompt that produces usable work instead of a confetti cannon of plausible sentences.

G

Goal

Tell AI what you are trying to accomplish, not just what you want it to produce. The goal helps it choose the right level of detail, tone, and reasoning path.

Try: “Create a one-page explainer for non-technical managers.”
C

Context

Give the background that changes the answer: audience, source material, history, current draft, business reason, known issue, or decision point.

Try: “This is for an internal AI education site for beginners.”
!

Constraints

State what must be included, avoided, preserved, protected, or treated carefully. Constraints are the guardrails, not decorative tape.

Try: “Do not include legal advice. Flag anything that needs verification.”
E

Examples

Provide a sample, rough draft, preferred tone, bad version, good version, or known pattern. Examples reduce ambiguity faster than a paragraph of adjectives.

Try: “Use this style: direct, practical, with short scenario cards.”
F

Output format

Specify the shape of the answer: table, checklist, executive summary, email, JSON, outline, decision matrix, or step-by-step plan.

Try: “Return a table with risk, recommendation, and review owner.”

The prompt-building loop

Use this loop when the answer matters enough that someone else will read, act on, approve, or challenge it.

Name the task

Use a strong verb: draft, review, compare, summarize, explain, improve, plan, extract, or challenge.

Add the situation

Explain who the answer is for, what they know, why it matters, and what source material should control the response.

Fence the work

List requirements, non-goals, forbidden claims, privacy limits, tone boundaries, and facts that must not be invented.

Show a pattern

Give an example of the desired style, structure, level of detail, or a previous answer you want matched or avoided.

Choose the shape

Tell AI whether the answer should be bullets, table, checklist, email, script, summary, rubric, or HTML section.

Ask for review points

Request assumptions, missing information, risks, verification points, and questions before anyone relies on the result.

The reusable prompt skeleton

This structure works for everyday tasks because it separates the work from the review criteria. It also makes the answer easier to fix when the first draft misses the mark.

Copy-ready 
Goal:
[What are we trying to accomplish?]

Context:
[Who is this for?  What background, source material, or situation matters?]

Constraints:
[What must be included, avoided, protected, preserved, or handled carefully?]

Examples:
[Optional: paste a sample, style reference, rough draft, good version, or bad version.]

Output format:
[Table, checklist, email, executive summary, outline, step-by-step plan, etc.]

Review requirements:
Before the final answer, list assumptions, missing information, claims to verify, and risks if this is used without review.

Filled example

Notice that the prompt does not ask AI to be the authority. It asks AI to produce reviewable material and identify what a person should check.

Example 
Goal:
Create a beginner-friendly explanation of why AI answers need human review.

Context:
This is for an internal AI education page.  The audience includes business users, technical staff, managers, and AI champions.

Constraints:
Keep it practical.  Do not use scare tactics.  Do not imply AI is useless.  Explain that confidence is not evidence.  Include verification habits.

Examples:
Use concrete workplace examples, such as summaries, customer messages, project plans, and policy explanations.

Output format:
Return four sections: key idea, good uses, risks, and review checklist.  Use short paragraphs and a final takeaway.

Review requirements:
List assumptions and any claims that should be verified before publishing.

Prompt upgrades in practice

The easiest way to improve AI output is to stop asking for “a thing” and start asking for “a thing that serves this purpose under these conditions.”

Summarizing a document

Weak: “Summarize this.”
Better: “Summarize this for a manager who needs decisions, risks, owners, and open questions. Separate confirmed points from assumptions, and flag anything that should be verified in the source text.”

Writing an email

Weak: “Write a nice email about the delay.”
Better: “Draft a calm internal email explaining that the deployment is delayed. Do not blame anyone. Include what changed, what happens next, and what details I must confirm before sending.”

Learning a concept

Weak: “Explain prompt injection.”
Better: “Explain prompt injection to beginner business users. Use one simple analogy, one workplace example, three warning signs, and a short checklist for what not to paste into AI tools.”

Planning work

Weak: “Make a plan for this project.”
Better: “Create a phased plan for this project. Include scope, non-goals, assumptions, dependencies, validation steps, risks, owners, and a stop point after each phase for human review.”

When the answer is bad

Bad output is often a prompt diagnostic. Do not just ask “try again.” Tell the AI what failed and tighten the missing ingredient.

Too generic

Add audience, situation, source material, and the decision the answer should support.
  • “Assume the reader is new to this.”
  • “Focus on what a manager needs to decide.”
  • “Use examples from internal workplace use, not consumer apps.”

Too long or wandering

Add a format, length target, section list, and priority order.
  • “Use five bullets maximum.”
  • “Start with the recommendation.”
  • “Put details in a table after the summary.”

Too confident

Ask for assumptions, uncertainty, missing facts, and verification points.
  • “Separate facts from interpretation.”
  • “Mark anything that requires a source check.”
  • “Do not invent dates, citations, policy text, or approvals.”

Fast ingredient matrix

Use this table when teaching someone why their prompt produced a fog machine instead of a flashlight.

Ingredient Question it answers What happens when it is missing
Goal What should this help the human accomplish? The answer may be technically correct but useless for the actual decision or audience.
Context What background changes the answer? The AI fills gaps with generic assumptions, which can quietly bend the result away from reality.
Constraints What must be included, avoided, protected, or preserved? The answer may create commitments, expose sensitive details, ignore policy, or overstep the task.
Examples What does good, bad, too formal, too casual, too vague, or just right look like? The AI guesses at tone, structure, level of detail, and what you mean by words like “professional” or “simple.”
Output format What shape makes this easiest to review? The answer may be a polished wall of text when you needed a checklist, table, script, or decision matrix.
Review requirements What should a person check before using this? The answer may hide assumptions, uncertainty, unsupported claims, and risks under confident prose.
Field guide takeaway: Better prompts create better review surfaces. Give AI the goal, context, constraints, examples, and output format. Then ask it to expose assumptions and verification points so the human reviewer can do the part only a human can own.
