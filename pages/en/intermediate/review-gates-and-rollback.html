Review Gates and Rollback

Review gates keep AI-assisted work safe by forcing visible checkpoints before changes reach users, systems, data, or production workflows.

Who this is for

This guide is for people who need AI-assisted work to pass through review, testing, approval, and recovery steps before it is trusted.

What you will learn

  • Which gates reduce AI-assisted delivery risk.
  • How rollback changes the way you review work.
  • What stopping conditions should be explicit.

Useful review gates

Diff review

Check exactly what changed and why.

Tests

Run automated checks that match the changed behavior.

Linting

Catch syntax, style, and formatting problems early.

Approvals

Require the right people to sign off.

Feature flags

Separate deployment from user exposure when possible.

Dry runs

Preview infrastructure, migration, or operational changes.

Rollback instructions

Write the recovery path before release.

Audit trail

Keep a record of prompts, changes, validation, and approvals.

Stopping conditions

Name the signals that pause or cancel the change.

Good use and risky use

Good use

Require review gates before release, confirm validation evidence, and document rollback steps before the change affects users or systems.

Risky use

Accept an AI summary as proof, skip diff review, deploy without dry runs, or discover rollback steps only after a change fails.

Try this workflow

Use this before approving a change.

1. Review the diff, not just the summary. 2. Run the agreed validation commands. 3. Confirm approval owners. 4. Confirm feature flag, dry-run, or staged rollout options. 5. Write rollback instructions. 6. Define stopping conditions. 7. Keep evidence in the ticket, commit, PR, or release notes.

Key takeaway

Review gates are not friction for their own sake. They make AI-assisted changes observable, reversible, and accountable.
