Risk Review Prompt

A reusable prompt for mapping blast radius, assumptions, approval gates, monitoring, rollback, and stop conditions before AI-assisted work touches real systems, data, money, users, public workflows, or organizational commitments.

Core Skill: Reuse the prompt Use: Monitoring and rollback Rule: Assumptions and gates Risk: Blast radius

What the prompt forces into daylight

A risk review is a pre-flight check for consequences. It should expose what can break, who can be affected, what must be approved, how failure will be detected, and how the team stops before the raccoon finds the wiring.

1

Proposed action

Name the action, decision, workflow, prompt, agent task, automation, release, or operational change being reviewed. Risk cannot be assessed against a fog machine.

2

Blast radius

Identify users, systems, data, money, security controls, vendors, public communications, operations, and support channels that could be affected.

3

Assumptions

Separate known facts from guesses, stale context, missing information, inherited constraints, and places where the AI may have filled silence with wallpaper.

4

Approval gates

Define who must approve before action, especially for security, privacy, legal, architecture, production, finance, brand, customer, or leadership impact.

5

Monitoring

Decide how success and failure will be seen: logs, dashboards, alerts, user reports, audit events, metrics, screenshots, test evidence, and support signals.

6

Rollback

Explain how to retreat, who can execute the rollback, what data cannot be reversed, and what must be captured before anything gets unwound.

7

Stop conditions

List conditions that require pausing, escalating, refusing, or switching to an approved path. A stop sign is most useful before the cliff.

8

Residual risk

State what risk remains after mitigation, who accepts it, what evidence supports that acceptance, and what must not be delegated to AI.

The practical rule

Use this prompt before any AI-assisted action that could affect real people, real systems, real money, private data, production behavior, legal obligations, public statements, security posture, or operational trust. It is not meant to stop useful work. It is meant to keep useful work from sprinting blindfolded through the server room.

Review the risk before the action, not after the smoke starts naming itself.

Copy-ready prompt

Replace bracketed text with your situation. Keep the stop, approval, and rollback sections when the work may affect systems, data, users, money, security, or public workflows.

Risk scaffold 
Role:
Act as a careful AI risk review assistant.  Your job is to evaluate the proposed action before it happens.  Do not approve the action yourself.  Do not assume missing approvals, hidden permissions, safe data handling, working rollback, or production readiness.

Goal:
Create a practical risk review for [proposed AI-assisted action, automation, code change, content change, workflow, data task, system change, communication, integration, or decision] so [person or team] can decide whether to proceed, revise, escalate, or stop.

Proposed action:
[Describe what will be done]

Context:
Current state: [what exists today]
Desired outcome: [what should be true afterward]
Who or what is affected: [users, teams, customers, systems, data, money, public pages, workflows, vendors, etc.]
AI role: [drafting, reviewing, coding, executing commands, calling tools, analyzing data, sending communication, triggering automation, etc.]
Human role: [reviewer, approver, operator, owner, accountable decision-maker]
Tools or systems involved: [AI tools, repositories, SaaS platforms, databases, cloud accounts, ticketing, CI/CD, communication tools, etc.]
Data involved: [classification, sensitivity, customer data, employee data, secrets, logs, financial data, production data, public data, placeholders, etc.]
Known constraints: [policy, security, privacy, legal, compliance, budget, timing, architecture, brand, accessibility, support, etc.]
Known approvals already obtained: [name approvals, or write none]
Known protected areas: [systems, files, data, workflows, users, decisions, or communications that must not be touched]
Source material: [paste requirements, plan, draft, prompt, ticket, proposed command, code summary, meeting note, or decision text]

Risk review constraints:
- Separate facts, assumptions, missing information, and recommendations.
- Do not invent approvals, owners, policy exceptions, deadlines, budgets, access, or test results.
- Treat AI as an assistant, not as the accountable approver.
- Identify what can break, who notices, and how quickly damage can spread.
- Call out any use of secrets, credentials, private data, regulated data, customer data, employee data, payment data, production data, or public-facing communication.
- Flag prompt injection, data leakage, permission misuse, hallucinated facts, stale context, automation loops, and irreversible actions where relevant.
- Identify approval gates before action, during action, and before production or public release.
- Include monitoring, alerting, rollback, and stop conditions.
- If the action should not proceed with the current information, say so clearly and explain what is needed.

Output format:
Return the risk review with these sections:

1. Executive risk snapshot
   - Proposed action
   - Risk level: low, medium, high, or critical
   - Why this level was assigned
   - Recommended decision: proceed, proceed with controls, revise, escalate, or stop
   - Highest-risk issue
   - Required approvals before action

2. Blast radius map
   - People affected
   - Systems affected
   - Data affected
   - Money, billing, procurement, or financial exposure
   - Public, customer, legal, brand, or communications exposure
   - Operational or support impact
   - Security, privacy, compliance, or audit impact

3. Facts, assumptions, and missing information
   - Known facts
   - Assumptions that must be confirmed
   - Missing context
   - Contradictions or stale information
   - Questions that block action
   - Questions that can proceed with documented assumptions

4. Risk register
   For each risk include:
   - Risk statement
   - Cause
   - Impact
   - Likelihood
   - Severity
   - Detection method
   - Mitigation
   - Owner or accountable reviewer
   - Escalation path
   - Residual risk after mitigation

5. Approval gates
   - Approvals required before action
   - Approvals required before production, public release, external communication, or automation
   - Security, privacy, legal, compliance, architecture, finance, brand, or leadership review needed
   - Evidence approvers should inspect
   - Decision that must remain human-owned

6. Monitoring and detection
   - Success signals
   - Failure signals
   - Logs, dashboards, alerts, metrics, screenshots, tickets, audit events, or reports to watch
   - Who watches them
   - Monitoring window
   - Thresholds that trigger escalation

7. Rollback and recovery
   - Rollback trigger conditions
   - Rollback steps
   - Who can execute rollback
   - Data reversal limits or irreversible effects
   - Communication needed if rollback happens
   - Evidence to capture before rollback
   - Recovery validation after rollback

8. Stop conditions
   List conditions that require an immediate pause, escalation, refusal, or approved-path change, including:
   - Missing approval
   - Unexpected production impact
   - Sensitive data exposure
   - Permission or access surprise
   - Unverified fact that affects a decision
   - Test failure
   - Monitoring alert
   - User harm, customer impact, financial exposure, security issue, or legal concern

9. Safer alternatives
   - Lower-risk way to achieve the goal
   - Placeholder or sanitized-data option
   - Manual review option
   - Dry-run, read-only, sandbox, pilot, or feature-flag option
   - Smaller checkpointed phase
   - Approved tool or approved workflow option

10. Final recommendation
   - Recommended next action
   - Whether work should proceed now
   - What must happen first
   - What should be documented
   - Who should review or approve

Review criteria:
Before finalizing, check whether the risk review:
- Names the blast radius clearly
- Does not hide uncertainty
- Does not treat AI output as approval
- Identifies approval gates
- Includes monitoring and stop conditions
- Explains rollback and recovery limits
- Flags sensitive data, secrets, permissions, automation, public exposure, and irreversible actions
- Gives humans enough information to approve, reject, revise, or escalate

Task:
Create the risk review from the material below.

Material:
[paste proposed action and context here]

When to use it

Use the risk prompt when the AI output may become an action, not just a thought. The more real the consequence, the more explicit the controls should become.

Before command execution Use it before shell commands, cloud actions, database operations, migrations, CI/CD triggers, infrastructure changes, or anything that can alter state.
Before external communication Use it before sending customer updates, public statements, policy messages, executive summaries, legal-sensitive language, or promises that bind a team.
Before sensitive-data work Use it when prompts, files, logs, exports, screenshots, or examples include customer, employee, financial, private, regulated, or security-sensitive information.
Before AI agents act Use it when an agent can edit files, call tools, create tickets, open pull requests, run tests, deploy, or cross a repository boundary.
Before process changes Use it when AI guidance may change approvals, responsibilities, SLAs, support paths, escalation rules, or how people make decisions.
Before automation repeats Use it before scheduled jobs, recurring summaries, auto-labeling, auto-routing, auto-replies, deployment automation, or anything that can fail at scale.

Weak prompt

This sounds responsible, but it leaves too many gaps. The AI may produce a polite list of concerns without naming approvals, stop points, monitoring, or rollback.

Review the risks of this plan.
  • No action boundary.
  • No affected users, data, systems, or approval context.
  • No instruction to separate facts from assumptions.
  • No monitoring, rollback, or stop conditions.
  • No reminder that humans own approval and accountability.

Stronger prompt

This version gives the AI enough shape to map the danger zone without pretending to be the authority that approves the work.

Act as a careful AI risk review assistant.  Do not approve the action yourself.

Proposed action:
Use an AI coding agent to update a production-adjacent website workflow and open a pull request.

Context:
- The work may affect a public form, admin behavior, and notification routing.
- The agent may read repository files and edit code, but must not deploy.
- No secrets, customer data, or production logs may be pasted into the AI tool.
- DEV validation is required before production review.
- Human approval is required before merge.

Create a risk review with:
1. Executive risk snapshot
2. Blast radius map
3. Facts, assumptions, missing information, and blockers
4. Risk register with detection and mitigation
5. Approval gates
6. Monitoring and failure signals
7. Rollback and recovery plan
8. Stop conditions
9. Safer alternatives
10. Final recommendation

Flag sensitive data, permission surprises, prompt injection, irreversible changes, public impact, and anything that requires security, privacy, architecture, or leadership review.

Stop conditions worth naming explicitly

The prompt should not merely say “be careful.” It should produce hard stop points that humans can recognize while the work is still containable.

Approval missing The work needs security, privacy, production, legal, finance, brand, architecture, or customer approval that has not been granted.
Data surprise The work exposes secrets, private data, production logs, customer records, employee data, payment details, or regulated information.
Scope escape The task starts touching files, systems, permissions, users, workflows, or decisions outside the agreed boundary.
No safe retreat The rollback path is unclear, untested, blocked by data irreversibility, or owned by nobody.

Review before the output becomes a decision

A risk review is not useful because it is long. It is useful when it changes what people do next: proceed with controls, revise the plan, escalate for approval, or stop.

Risk level logic Check whether the risk rating is justified by impact, likelihood, reversibility, sensitivity, visibility, and automation scale.
Control quality Mitigations should be concrete, assigned, testable, and visible. “Be careful” is not a control, it is a decorative pothole sign.
Human ownership Approval, accountability, external communication, final decisions, and risk acceptance must remain human-owned.
Evidence trail The review should say what evidence must be captured: test results, approvals, screenshots, logs, plans, tickets, or monitoring snapshots.

Prompt-library takeaway: A risk review prompt turns “what could go wrong?” into a usable control surface. It names blast radius, assumptions, approval gates, monitoring, rollback, and stop conditions before the action begins, when humans can still steer instead of merely narrating the crater.
