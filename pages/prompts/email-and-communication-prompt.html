Email and Communication Prompt

Use AI to improve clarity and tone, but remember that the sender owns the message.

Expertise: Beginner Category: Prompt Library Audience: Business communicators Use when: Drafting or improving a message Version: 1.0
Back to index Previous: Prompting Basics Next: Document Review

Who this is for

This prompt is for employees drafting emails, announcements, customer updates, internal notes, and follow-up messages.

When to use this

Use it when you know the facts and want help with structure, tone, clarity, length, or a stronger call to action.

When not to use this

Do not use it to invent commitments, change approved facts, make legal or policy claims without review, or include confidential recipient details in unapproved tools.

Copy-ready prompt templates

Draft or improve a message 
Draft or improve this communication.

Audience:
[AUDIENCE]

Goal:
[GOAL]

Tone:
[professional / warm / concise / executive / customer-facing]

Facts that must stay unchanged:
- [FACT 1]
- [FACT 2]
- [FACT 3]

Call to action:
[WHAT THE READER SHOULD DO]

Length:
[LENGTH]

Things to avoid:
- [THING TO AVOID 1]
- [THING TO AVOID 2]

Return:
- a polished draft
- a short subject line if relevant
- any assumptions or risks I should review before sending
Tone variants 
Rewrite the message in these variants without changing the facts:
1. Professional
2. Warm
3. Concise
4. Executive
5. Customer-facing

For each version, explain when it is appropriate and what risk I should check before sending.
Sender accountability: Check recipients, facts, promises, privacy, approvals, and tone before sending. AI does not know your full business context.

How to adapt it

Audience: Name the reader group and their likely concern. Goal: Say whether you need action, awareness, alignment, or reassurance. Facts: Lock facts that must not change. Tone: Pick a tone that matches the relationship and situation. Length: Request a word count or bullet count. Avoid: Name phrases, commitments, or sensitive details that should not appear.

Review before using the output

Accuracy: Verify every fact, date, name, and commitment. Tone: Confirm it sounds appropriate for the audience. Privacy: Remove private or confidential details. Approvals: Check whether policy, customer, legal, or leadership review is required. Action: Make sure the call to action is clear and authorized. Ownership: Edit the final message in your own judgment.

Key takeaway

AI can make communication clearer, but it cannot take responsibility for the facts, tone, audience, or business commitment.
