Executive Summary Prompt

Use this when leadership needs decisions, risks, owners, and next steps, not a pile of raw detail.

Expertise: Beginner Also useful for: Intermediate Category: Prompt Library Audience: Managers, analysts, and team leads Version: 1.0
Back to index Previous: Document Review Next: Meeting Summary

Who this is for

This prompt is for people turning detailed notes, findings, proposals, or project updates into leadership-ready summaries.

When to use this

Use it when a reader needs the point, the decision, the options, the risk, the owner, and the next action quickly.

When not to use this

Do not use it to hide uncertainty, compress unresolved conflict into false agreement, or present unverified numbers as facts.

Copy-ready prompt template

Executive summary 
Turn the following information into an executive summary.

Audience:
[LEADERSHIP AUDIENCE]

Context:
[BACKGROUND]

Source material:
[PASTE REDACTED NOTES OR DETAILS]

Return:
1. One-paragraph summary
2. Decision needed
3. Options
4. Recommendation
5. Risks
6. Cost or effort
7. Timeline
8. Owner
9. Open questions

Keep it concise. Separate facts from assumptions. Flag numbers, dates, names, or claims that need verification.
Verify before sending: Numbers, dates, names, claims, timelines, costs, and recommendations must be checked against source material and accountable owners.

How to adapt it

Audience: Identify whether readers are executives, sponsors, approvers, or steering committee members. Decision: Say what decision or action is needed. Options: Ask for two or three options when tradeoffs matter. Recommendation: Require reasoning and assumptions, not just a conclusion. Risks: Include business, delivery, security, financial, and people risks where relevant. Length: Set a word limit if the audience expects brevity.

Review before using the output

Facts: Check all names, dates, numbers, and status claims. Recommendation: Confirm the recommendation matches the evidence. Risk: Make sure material risks are not softened away. Owner: Confirm named owners accept the work. Decision: Make sure the ask is specific. Gaps: Keep open questions visible.

Key takeaway

A useful executive summary does not just shorten text. It clarifies the decision, tradeoffs, risks, owner, and next step.
